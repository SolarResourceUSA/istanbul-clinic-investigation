5. Top Mid-Facelift Options

Critical: do not shop the word "mid-facelift." For a 50-yo man, an isolated midface lift is frequently the wrong operation.

What a 50-yo man is usually actually a candidate for. A true isolated mid-facelift lifts only the cheek fat pad and does not touch the jawline or neck — which is exactly what reads as "aged" in male faces. Most men this age are better served by a deep-plane (or SMAS) facelift that repositions midface + jowl + upper neck as one unit, often combined with a neck lift ± lower-lid blepharoplasty. Jeremy should not pre-diagnose — he should ask each surgeon in person: "Given my anatomy, what plane and which zones, and is a 'mid-facelift' even right for me?"
Procedure glossary — what each actually addresses
  • Isolated / vertical midface lift: cheek fat pad only; not jowls/neck.
  • Endoscopic midface lift: scarless, for younger faces / mild descent (Tezel, Avşar specialize).
  • SMAS facelift: workhorse; tightens the SMAS over lower face/jowl; predictable, lower-risk dissection.
  • Deep-plane / extended deep-plane: releases retaining ligaments; midface + jowl + jawline (+ neck) in one; ~10–15 yr longevity.
  • Mini / lower facelift: jowl/lower face only, shorter scar.
  • Neck lift: platysmaplasty ± submental lipo — often the highest-value add for a man at 50.
  • Fat grafting / blepharoplasty: common adjuncts for volume / eyes.

6. Top Skinny-BBL Options (Vanessa)

Safety is the entire story. BBL has the highest mortality of any cosmetic procedure.

First, the honest candidacy question. A real "skinny BBL" = liposuction + transfer of the patient's own fat, subcutaneous plane only, smaller volume, shape-focused. It needs donor fat — roughly ~1,000 ml harvested to yield ~500–600 ml purified per side. If Vanessa is very lean (body fat under ~18–20% with little pinchable fat), she may not be a candidate, and a safe surgeon will say so or offer only a very modest result. Have her donor fat honestly assessed before anything else. The right answer may be "no" — not a pivot to fillers.
Hard disqualifiers — reject outright. Any injectable silicone, hydrogel, biopolymer, or large-volume permanent filler (disfiguring, unremovable). Any surgeon who won't name themselves or won't name the injected material. Any who won't confirm real-time ultrasound-guided, subcutaneous-only (above the muscle) fat placement. Non-hospital settings. More than ~3 BBLs/day.
Note: some Istanbul clinics market "skinny BBL" but pivot lean patients to Sculptra/PLLA, Radiesse, or "AlloClae" — these are fillers/biostimulators, not a fat-transfer BBL. Treat any such pivot as a yellow flag.

The non-negotiable safety questions (all "yes," in writing)

  • My own fat only — no silicone/hydrogel/biopolymer/permanent filler?
  • Fat placed subcutaneously only, above the gluteal muscle/fascia? (Every confirmed BBL death involved deep/intramuscular fat.)
  • Real-time intraoperative ultrasound tracking the cannula throughout? (ASPS/ASAPS/ISAPS/BAAPS standard since 2022.)
  • You, the named surgeon, perform the entire operation, with a dedicated anesthesiologist present throughout?
  • JCI-accredited hospital, overnight monitoring, VTE/blood-clot prophylaxis, ≤3 BBLs/day, no fly for ~7–10 days?
